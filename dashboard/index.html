Sydney Value Buys

No sales data yet. The map, transport, parks, schools, crime and rates tabs work from the reference feeds. Land $/sqm, street tables, drawdowns and betas need the NSW Valuer General sales files:
  1. Open valuation.property.nsw.gov.au - Property Sales Information, pick Yearly, download 2012 to the current year.
  2. Save them as raw\vg\2012.zip ... raw\vg\2026.zip.
  3. Run run_all.cmd in the project folder, then reload this page.

Composite = weighted average of Sydney-wide z-scores. Higher = better value for a buyer.
Move the sliders, then press Update rankings (or Enter). The lists re-sort with the best match at the top.
How the numbers work: each number (0–10) is how much that factor counts relative to the others. Only the proportions matter: the % beside each number is its share of the total, so "Gross yield 3 · 13%" means rental yield drives about 13% of a suburb's score. Raise a number to make that factor matter more, set it to 0 to ignore it. Doubling every slider changes nothing.

Strategy
Targets

Rent: median weekly rent for new house bonds in the postcode over the trailing 12 months (NSW rental bond lodgements), by bedroom count where 8+ bonds exist. Room rent = house rent ÷ bedrooms × (1 + premium); by the room = all rooms let at occupancy; live-in = all but one room let, at the owner-occupier rate, and "your weekly cost" is interest + costs − board. Price: suburb median house price (Valuer General where loaded, else DCJ postcode non-strata median, 4-quarter average); 3- and 4-bed prices are scaled from the median by the bedroom rent ratio because no public source gives price by bedrooms. Net yield = gross × (1 − costs). Cash ROE = (net yield − LVR × rate) ÷ (1 − LVR), after tax if a rate is set. ROE = cash ROE + CAGR ÷ (1 − LVR). First-year, before stamp duty, CGT and depreciation. Leave by: public transport from the Transport for NSW trip planner arriving Central by 9:00 on a Monday (needs the API key; blank otherwise); driving = free-flow OSRM time × peak factor. Lot: median sold lot where sales are loaded, else median residential parcel in the suburb from the NSW parcel layer. Score: your weighted composite, computed for every suburb here regardless of the filters on the Suburb ranking tab. Townhouse / villa: Valuer General strata-lot sales in schemes of up to 40 lots with at least 170 sqm of scheme land per lot (smaller = flats, excluded); price = trailing-12m median of those sales (5+), rent = townhouse bonds in the postcode, "Lot" = land per lot, CAGR from the same sales 3/5/10 years earlier (8+). Peak, $/sqm and re-rating columns apply to houses only.

Factor premiums: market vs yours

"Market pays" is what Sydney buyers actually paid for each factor, estimated from sales (see Method), and is already inside the fair value. Each slider starts at that market rate. Slide it left if you would pay more than the market for that factor, right if less; the last column shows what that does to this property, which is only ever the difference between your rate and the market's, applied to how far the property sits from the Sydney average. The result is the "value with your premiums" figure at the top.

Sources

Sales
NSW Valuer General Property Sales Information (bulk, weekly/yearly). Contract price, land area, zoning, nature of property. Units (strata) excluded.
Parcels
NSW Spatial Services Land Parcel & Property theme, joined on the Valuer General property ID: parcel centroid and polygon area.
Stations
NSW Spatial Services Features of Interest: train and metro stations (operational).
Schools
Two separate scores. Public (catchment): the nearest government high school you can attend by address (NSW Education school list; fully selective, private and audition-only schools excluded), scored by its relative position on 5-year average HSC rank (2021-2025 top-150 success-rate tables; #1 of all schools = 100, unranked = 0). Nearest by distance, not the official catchment boundary. Private (nearby): no catchment, so what counts is the drop-off run: each ranked private school's score counts in full within 1.5 km and halves every 2 km beyond; the suburb gets the average of its two best. Scores live in raw/ref/school_scores.csv (type high / selective / private). Primary schools are still distance-only.
Parks
NSW Planning Portal land zoning: RE1 Public Recreation parcels of 3,000 sqm or more.
Crime
BOCSAR recorded crime by suburb, monthly. Violent = assault, robbery, sexual offences. Property = break and enter dwelling, motor vehicle theft, steal from motor vehicle, steal from dwelling, malicious damage. Per 1,000 residents, 2021 Census (ABS SAL).
Rates
RBA F1.1 cash rate target (monthly average) and F5 bank housing lending rates. ABS Residential Property Price Index (Greater Sydney) to 2021.
Rents
NSW Fair Trading rental bond lodgements, monthly: every new bond with postcode, dwelling type, bedrooms and weekly rent. Trailing 12 months, houses, 10+ bonds per postcode.
Postcode prices
DCJ Rent and Sales Report, sales tables by postcode: quarterly median non-strata sale price from the June 2017 quarter. Used for price and CAGRs until Valuer General sales are loaded.
Lot sizes
NSW Spatial Services Property layer: median and mean area of non-strata parcels between 150 and 5,000 sqm inside each suburb polygon (one statistics query per suburb, cached 90 days). Sold-lot medians replace it where sales are loaded.
Commute
Driving: OSRM public router, free-flow time from the suburb centroid to Central Station, multiplied by the peak factor on the Yield tab. Public transport: Transport for NSW Trip Planner, fastest journey arriving Central by 9:00 on the next Monday (requires a free API key; blank otherwise).
Fair value
Per suburb, houses sold in the last 5 years: ln(price) = a + b·ln(land sqm), with each sale first adjusted to today using the suburb's rolling 12-month median (Sydney's where the suburb is thin). b is clipped to 0.15–0.9 and needs 20 sales. Street effect = median residual of the street's sales (3+), shrunk by n÷(n+3) toward zero. Fair value for a block of L sqm on street S = exp(a + b·ln L + effect); the range uses the 25th and 75th percentile residuals of the suburb. "vs fair" on a sale = price ÷ fair value at the time. No building attributes are in the public data, so condition, size and views sit inside the residual. Amenity premiums (Fair value tab): the stage-1 residuals are regressed, pooled across Sydney, on km to the nearest station, park, primary and high school (each capped, and demeaned within the suburb so only within-suburb variation identifies the premium); the street effect is taken after this adjustment. Suburb level: the value of a 600 sqm lot is regressed across suburbs on km to the CBD, public-transport minutes to Central and offences per 1,000; the residual is the suburb's unexplained premium. "Your value" replaces the market premium on any factor with yours, applied to the property's difference from the Sydney average. The address lookup uses the Valuer General records themselves (houses sold since 2012, located through the NSW property layer), so an address that has not sold since 2012 falls back to the suburb centroid.
Townhouse / villa
Valuer General strata residence sales are grouped by property ID (one per strata scheme). Lots in the scheme = the highest unit number or the distinct units sold since 2012, whichever is larger; scheme land = the NSW Property-layer polygon area. Schemes with 40 or fewer lots and 170+ sqm of land per lot are treated as townhouses or villas, the rest as flats. Rents are NSW rental bond lodgements with dwelling type T (townhouse) in the postcode. No public source labels townhouses directly, so this is a land-per-lot heuristic; a block of eight 1960s walk-up flats on 1,400 sqm sits right at the threshold.
Loan rates
RBA table F6, rates on new housing loans funded in the month (variable, all institutions), investor and owner-occupier. Type over the rate to test a hike; the RBA button restores it.
Peaks and drawdowns
Rolling 12-month pooled medians (15+ sales) of land $/sqm and of price per suburb from 2012; peak = highest rolling year, "now" = latest rolling year. Sydney peak on the same basis. "vs Sydney" = suburb drawdown minus Sydney drawdown. "Re-rating" = price change since the suburb's price peak minus the postcode's house rent change over the same period (DCJ rent tables): negative means the price fell relative to rents. The Price window selector swaps the current price for the last 6, 3 or 2 months (5+ sales), and "Price vs peak" uses that window.
Socio-economic index
ABS SEIFA Index of Relative Socio-economic Advantage and Disadvantage (IRSAD) from the 2021 Census by suburb: income, education, occupation and housing measures combined; 1000 = national average. Two sliders: Well-off area today is the 2021 level; Moving up is the change in the suburb's national percentile between the 2016 and 2021 Censuses, which rewards suburbs on the way up rather than ones already at the top (a suburb at the 100th percentile scores about average on it). Suburb boundaries changed slightly between censuses, and the next update will come from the August 2026 Census, whose SEIFA results are not out yet.
Infrastructure
Hand-maintained list of committed projects (raw/ref/infrastructure.csv: Metro West, Western Sydney Airport metro and airport, Parramatta Light Rail stage 2, Western Harbour Tunnel, hospitals) plus the Transport Oriented Development sites and accelerated precincts from the Housing SEPP 2021 map layers. Score = Σ weight × (1 − km/3) for projects within 3 km of the suburb centroid, recent (already opened) projects at half weight. Edit the CSV to add or re-weight projects.
Neighbour gap
ln(suburb $/sqm ÷ inverse-distance-weighted mean $/sqm of suburbs within 4 km). Negative = cheaper than its surroundings, one of the classic precursors of gentrification.
Gentrification pressure
Weighted z-scores: cheaper than neighbours (35%), 3-year rent CAGR minus 3-year price CAGR (25%), fall in offences versus 5 years ago (15%), infrastructure score (15%), proximity to the CBD (10%); the weighted sum is z-scored again. Needs at least the neighbour gap and 60% of the weights. It flags the rent-gap set-up that precedes gentrification, not whether it has started; check the suburb page for the parts.

Definitions

Land $/sqm
Contract price divided by Valuer General land area, houses and vacant residential land only, lots 120 to 5,000 sqm, price at least $150k, $/sqm between 150 and 60,000.
12m medians
Trailing twelve months from the latest contract date in the data (shown in the header). Prior 12 months for the year-on-year change.
Value (ring residual)
Fit ln(median $/sqm) = a + b·km + c·km² across suburbs with 10+ sales, then residual = ln(actual / fitted). Negative residual = cheaper than its distance ring implies. The composite uses minus the residual.
Drawdown
Latest 2-quarter pooled median $/sqm versus the highest such value in 2021-2022. Pooled quarters need 8+ sales.
Beta
OLS slope of the 2-quarter log change in the pooled median on the 2-quarter change in the quarterly-average cash rate, from 2010, needs 16+ overlapping observations. R² shown alongside; treat betas with low R² and few sales as noise.
Composite
Σ weight × z-score ÷ Σ weight, over the components available for that suburb. Z-scores clipped at ±2.5. Distances are log-transformed before scoring.

Caveats

  • Sales appear in the VG file at settlement, so the latest 1 to 3 months are incomplete. Contract date is used throughout.
  • $/sqm ignores the building: a knock-down on a big block and a new build on the same block both count. That is deliberate for a land-value screen, but check zoning (R2 vs R3/R4) and the recent-sales table before reading too much into a street.
  • Suburb polygons and Census names are matched by name; a few suburbs lack population (crime rate blank) or straddle postcodes.
  • Distances are straight-line from the parcel centroid, not walking routes.
  • Nothing here is advice. Public data, personal research.

Refresh

Download the latest Valuer General yearly zip into raw\vg\ (overwrite the current year), then run run_all.cmd. Reference feeds refresh every run. First geocode pass is slow (one request per 400 properties); later runs only look up new IDs.
